### The one killer case, in one breath
If you only land one technical idea, land this: **"The voice was perfect. The table was never
booked. Everyone else grades the words — we grade what it actually did."** That single
said-vs-did flip is the whole taxonomy, made legible in five seconds.

## Full spoken script

Local businesses lose customers for a simple reason: someone calls, and no one picks up.

A missed call can be a table not booked, a patient not scheduled, a job not sent out, or a
client who calls someone else.

Local businesses feel this the most because their calls usually matter right now.

No one casually calls a plumber. They need help now.

No one calls a restaurant for fun. They want a table, an order, a change, or a refund.

The phone is where customers ask for the thing they need.

The obvious fix is an AI phone agent. But owners do not trust it. And they are right not to.

A missed call is bad. A wrong answer is worse. Promise the wrong allergy info, book a sold-out
table, mishandle a refund, or give bad clinic instructions, and now the AI did damage.

That is Otto.

The simplest possible front door for a local business: give us your website and phone number.

We build the agent, connect the line, and learn the business automatically.

But the real product is what happens before and after it goes live.

First, Otto runs a pre-launch eval harness with Cekura.

As soon as the business gives us its website, we generate a voice agent, then Cekura
attacks it with synthetic callers tailored to that business.

For a restaurant, that means allergies, large parties, sold-out tables, bad audio, impatient callers.

For a contractor, clinic, salon, or law firm, the tests change automatically.

Cekura finds the failures, Otto patches the policy, reruns the evals, and the phone line only
goes live after it clears the safety gate.

So the customer does almost nothing. The system does the hard part: build, test, fix, verify.

Second, Otto keeps running in production.

Every real call becomes an event stream: what the caller said, what the agent said, what tools
it called, what happened, and whether the outcome was actually correct.

That matters because the voice can sound perfect while the business outcome is wrong.

The agent can say, "you're booked," when the table was never booked.

When Otto detects that, it spins up variations of the exact production failure: angry caller,
rushed caller, accented caller, noisy line, interruptions, language switching.

Cekura runs those variations, Otto fixes the root cause, and verifies the fix does not break
anything that already worked.

So for the customer, Otto is simple: website in, phone number connected, AI answers.

Behind the scenes, Cekura breaks it before customers do, and Otto keeps healing it after launch.

We are not selling another voice bot.

We are selling local businesses the confidence to finally let AI pick up the phone.

## Recording setup

**Part A (on camera).** Shoot loose — walking, real, two people. Phone-in-hand is good B-roll.
The numbers above are the script spine, not a teleprompter; say them in your own words but keep
the tags honest (don't upgrade a [T2] to "studies show", don't put a dollar figure on the
[logic] stakes line). Record clean audio — this half lives or dies on it.

**Part B (screen + call).**
1. `./scripts/dev.sh` → `http://localhost:8000` (full-screen, clean browser).
2. Have the **Piccino** chip ready; do a dry run so fonts/animation are warm.
3. For the live call: agent running (`uv run bot.py --transport twilio --proxy <ngrok>`),
   Twilio number on speaker, quiet room. If wifi is risky, pre-record this segment.
4. Screen-record at 60fps; the **self-heal 58→100 count-up** and the **OUTCOME×critical
   red→green** are the hero shots — give each a beat to breathe.

## Show the breadth (verbally, not built)

After the restaurant run, click the contractor, clinic, salon, and law-firm chips: same
flow, totally different synthetic callers (emergency dispatch / licensing for the
contractor; "no medical advice" / Rx / identity-verify for the clinic; specific-stylist /
dye-allergy for the salon; "no legal advice" / conflict-check for the law firm). One line:
**"Different business, same launch flow. Website in, safe phone line out."**
